§2Why it exists — How Long-Division Paralysis Ruins Interviews

Watch a candidate freeze in an interview when trying to divide `350,000,000 events / 86,400 seconds/day` by hand: 

// THE PAINFUL WAY (Candidate stares at whiteboard for 3 minutes doing long division):
350,000,000 / 86,400 = 350,000 / 86.4 = ... uh ... 4,050.9259? Or 40,509? (Drops a zero and panics!)

// THE POWERS-OF-10 WAY (Done in 4 seconds with zero chance of dropping a zero!):
350 Million = 3.5 * 10^8
1 Day       = 10^5 seconds
QPS         = (3.5 * 10^8) / (10^5) = 3.5 * 10^3 = 3,500 QPS! (Within 13% of exact—100% accurate for sizing boxes!)

§4When to use — 3 Rapid-Fire Back-of-the-Envelope Drills

Interview Question 15-Second Scientific-Notation Solution Architectural Verdict
"Can we keep a mapping of `user_id (8B) -> last_active_timestamp (8B)` for all 500 Million daily active users in RAM?" $(5 \times 10^8\text{ users}) \times (16\text{ B} + 32\text{ B hash-entry overhead}) \approx 5 \times 10^8 \times 50\text{ B} = 2.5 \times 10^{10}\text{ B} = \mathbf{25\text{ GB of RAM}}$. Yes! 25 GB fits easily on a single 64 GB Redis node (plus 1 replica for HA).
"4 Million Uber drivers send a GPS ping (`driver_id + lat + lon + timestamp = 32B`, or `~100B` with packet headers) every 4 seconds. What is QPS and RAM needed for current locations?" $\text{Write QPS} = \frac{4 \times 10^6}{4\text{ s}} = \mathbf{10^6\text{ writes/sec (1M QPS)}}$.
$\text{RAM for latest position of 4M drivers} = 4 \times 10^6 \times 100\text{ B} = \mathbf{400\text{ MB}}$.		 400 MB is tiny in RAM, but 1,000,000 writes/sec cannot hit a disk SQL DB—store live locations in a sharded in-memory Redis/Geohash cluster!
"A video service receives 1,000 hours of raw video uploaded per minute. After transcoding into 3 resolutions (avg 1 GB/hour total), how much daily storage is needed?" $1,000\text{ hr/min} \times 60\text{ min/hr} \times 24\text{ hr/day} = 1.44 \times 10^6\text{ hours/day}$.
$1.44 \times 10^6 \times 1\text{ GB} = \mathbf{1.44\text{ PB/day}}$.		 Needs Petabyte-scale Object Storage (S3) + lifecycle tiering to cold storage for unwatched videos.

§5Explain it to a 12-year-old (The Feynman Pass)

Why do we round `86,400 seconds` to `100,000 seconds`?

Imagine you are packing for a camping trip and ask: "Will all our gear fit in the trunk of Mom's sedan, or do we need to rent a moving truck?"

To answer that question, you do not need a laser ruler to measure whether your sleeping bag is `42.37 inches` or `45.00 inches` long! You just need to know if you are packing 3 backpacks (fits in the car = 1 PostgreSQL server) or 300 refrigerators (needs a convoy of 18-wheelers = 64 sharded servers + S3). Rounding to powers of 10 lets you spot "3 backpacks vs 300 refrigerators" in 5 seconds in your head!

§8Pitfalls & Traps

⚠️ Pitfall 1 — Storing UUIDs as 36-Character Strings Instead of 16-Byte Binary (`UUID` / `BIGINT`): At 10 Billion rows, a 36-byte string UUID costs `360 GB` in the primary key index alone (plus `360 GB` in every foreign key index!), whereas an 8-byte `BIGINT` Snowflake ID costs only `80 GB` and inserts sequentially into B+Tree pages without random page splits!
⚠️ Pitfall 2 — Forgetting Java Object Header Overhead in In-Memory Calculations: In a 64-bit JVM (`java/06-memory-model-and-gc`), every `new Object()` has a 12-to-16 byte object header, and a `HashMap.Node` adds 32 bytes per entry. Storing 100 million tiny 8-byte `Long` objects in a `HashMap` in JVM heap takes `~6–8 GB` of RAM, not `1.6 GB`! Use primitive arrays/off-heap buffers when memory density matters.

§9Recap & Quick Cheat-Sheet

  • Always Convert to Scientific Notation ($a \times 10^b$): Divide coefficients, subtract exponents, never lose a zero.
  • Golden Time Anchor: $1\text{ day} \approx 10^5\text{ seconds} \implies 100\text{M events/day} = 1,000\text{ QPS}$; $5\text{ years} \approx 2,000\text{ days}$.
  • Golden Latency Ladder: RAM (`100 ns`) $\to$ SSD (`100 \mu s` = $1,000\times$) $\to$ Same-AZ Network (`500 \mu s`) $\to$ Cross-AZ (`1.5 ms`) $\to$ Cross-Ocean (`100 ms` = $1,000,000\times$).
